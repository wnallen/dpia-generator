## Saudi Arabia — PDPL

DPIA-like assessment expected for high-risk processing under the PDPL (Royal Decree M/19,
2021, as amended) and SDAIA's implementing regulations; GDPR-shaped but with consent as the
dominant basis and a transfer regime run through SDAIA approvals/exceptions. Regulator: SDAIA.
Arabic authoritative text. *(2026-08-11: SDAIA consulted on amended Implementing Regulations
Apr–May 2025 — clarifying DPIA and controller-registration provisions; finalization
unconfirmed. SDAIA is actively enforcing (dozens of published violation decisions) and issued
an AI Adoption Framework (Nov 2025).)*

## UAE — Federal PDPL

Federal Decree-Law No. 45/2021 requires a DPIA (recalled Art. 21) for high-risk processing,
notably new technologies and large-scale sensitive data — but the **executive regulations
remained pending for years**; verify status before asserting anything operative. *(2026-08-11:
secondary sources report the Executive Regulations were **finally issued in 2026** by Cabinet
decision with a compliance runway into 2027 — sourcing is weak and the decision number
conflicts across reports; verify against the UAE official gazette before asserting.)*
Free-zone regimes (DIFC DP Law 2020, ADGM DPR 2021) have their own, more mature, DPIA
expectations and their own regulators — check which body of law actually applies to the
establishment.

## South Africa — POPIA

No express DPIA duty in POPIA itself; regulation 4(1)(b) under s. 55 requires the information
officer to ensure a "personal information impact assessment" is done. Prior authorisation from
the Information Regulator is required for enumerated processing categories (s. 57–58 —
unique identifiers, criminal data, credit reporting, transborder transfers of special personal
information of children) — a genuine stop-the-line mechanism closer to Art. 36 than most.

## Nigeria — NDPA 2023

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 requires a DPIA where processing is likely to result in
high risk (recalled s. 28), and the NDPC's General Application and Implementation Directive
(GAID, 2025) operationalizes it; data controllers "of major importance" carry registration and
audit obligations through licensed DPCOs. Regulator: NDPC. *(2026-08-11: GAID confirmed in
force — issued ~2025-03-20, **effective 2025-09-19**; the NDPR 2019 and its 2020
Implementation Framework no longer apply.)*

## Japan — APPI

No DPIA obligation; the PPC encourages PIAs as best practice and expects records of
third-party provision, purpose limitation, and anonymized/pseudonymized information handling.
Japan holds mutual adequacy with the EU — relevant to the EU limb's transfer row, not to a
Japanese assessment duty. *(2026-08-11: the APPI amendment from the triennial review **passed
the Diet 2026-07-10, promulgated 2026-07-17, not yet in force** (entry into force within two
years) — direct administrative monetary penalties, AI/data-utilization exceptions,
risk-tailored obligations; still no statutory PIA mandate.)*

## Thailand — PDPA

GDPR-shaped statute (2019, fully effective 2022) with a developing secondary-regulation
layer. **DPIAs are not generally mandatory** — no standalone mandatory-DPIA PDPC notification
was found by the 2026-08-11 pass (do not assert one); DPIA expectations arrive via guidance,
notably the PDPC's AI-related guidelines (effective 2026-03-25) calling for DPIAs for
high-risk AI. Also: DPO now mandatory for state agencies (Oct 2025); first major PDPC fines
issued Aug 2025. Verify the current notification set before asserting any mandatory trigger.

## Mexico — LFPDPPP (2025) / LGPDPPSO

A new **LFPDPPP** (private sector) was published 2025-03-20 and took effect 2025-03-21,
abrogating the 2010 law and dissolving INAI (supervision moved to the Secretaría
Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno); **no general private-sector PIA obligation** surfaced —
screen for security-measures and ARCO-rights obligations instead, and verify against the
statute text (implementing regulations pending). The public-sector **LGPDPPSO** does require
an **evaluación de impacto** submitted 30 days before deploying or materially modifying
public-facing systems — relevant only where the client is or serves a Mexican public body.
