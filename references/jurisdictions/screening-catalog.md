# Screening Catalog — Regimes Without Full Modules (Tier 3)

One-paragraph screening entries for regimes the skill can *name accurately* but has not built
out. These have **no regime code in the builder** — a processing touching one of them takes
the coverage fallback, upgraded by this catalog: instead of a bare "not covered", the DPIA's
Section 6 may carry the paragraph below (tagged `[model knowledge — verify]`) plus the
fallback sentence: *"Coverage of [regime] is limited to this screening note; its own
assessment obligation has not been analyzed."* Promote an entry to a full module when demand
arrives — never by patching prose here, but by the standard module build (fetch primary
sources, crosswalk, privilege posture, registry code, fixtures).

All entries UNVERIFIED as of 2026-08-04.

## Saudi Arabia — PDPL

DPIA-like assessment expected for high-risk processing under the PDPL (Royal Decree M/19,
2021, as amended) and SDAIA's implementing regulations; GDPR-shaped but with consent as the
dominant basis and a transfer regime run through SDAIA approvals/exceptions. Regulator: SDAIA.
Arabic authoritative text.

## UAE — Federal PDPL

Federal Decree-Law No. 45/2021 requires a DPIA (recalled Art. 21) for high-risk processing,
notably new technologies and large-scale sensitive data — but the **executive regulations
remained pending for years**; verify status before asserting anything operative. Free-zone
regimes (DIFC DP Law 2020, ADGM DPR 2021) have their own, more mature, DPIA expectations and
their own regulators — check which body of law actually applies to the establishment.

## South Africa — POPIA

No express DPIA duty in POPIA itself; regulation 4(1)(b) under s. 55 requires the information
officer to ensure a "personal information impact assessment" is done. Prior authorisation from
the Information Regulator is required for enumerated processing categories (s. 57–58 —
unique identifiers, criminal data, credit reporting, transborder transfers of special personal
information of children) — a genuine stop-the-line mechanism closer to Art. 36 than most.

## Nigeria — NDPA 2023

The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 requires a DPIA where processing is likely to result in
high risk (recalled s. 28), and the NDPC's General Application and Implementation Directive
(GAID, 2025) operationalizes it; data controllers "of major importance" carry registration and
audit obligations through licensed DPCOs. Regulator: NDPC.

## Kenya — Data Protection Act 2019

Express DPIA duty (s. 31) where processing is likely to result in high risk, with ODPC
guidance and a registration regime. One of the clearest GDPR-family DPIA duties in Africa;
promote early if African-scope work recurs.

## Japan — APPI

No DPIA obligation; the PPC encourages PIAs as best practice and expects records of
third-party provision, purpose limitation, and anonymized/pseudonymized information handling.
Japan holds mutual adequacy with the EU — relevant to the EU limb's transfer row, not to a
Japanese assessment duty.

## Thailand — PDPA

DPIA expected for high-risk processing via PDPC (Thailand) notifications and guidance;
GDPR-shaped statute (2019, fully effective 2022) with a developing secondary-regulation
layer. Verify the current notification set before asserting a mandatory trigger.
